Product Strategy Document Template Library
A companion to the PRD Template Library — but one level up. A PRD answers "what are we building and how." A strategy doc answers "why this, why now, why us, and what we're deliberately choosing not to do." Different altitude, different audience (often board/leadership, not just eng).

1. Lean Strategy One-Pager
Best for: early-stage, single team, needs to fit on one page and be referenced weekly.

PRODUCT/COMPANY: [Name]

TIME HORIZON: [e.g., next 2 quarters]

WHERE WE ARE

1-2 sentences — honest current state.

WHERE WE'RE GOING

1-2 sentences — the specific future state we're aiming for.

WHY THIS MATTERS NOW

The market/customer/business reason this is the right time.

HOW WE'LL GET THERE

3-5 bullet initiatives — not a full roadmap, the big rocks.

WHAT WE'RE NOT DOING

Explicit tradeoffs — the things a reasonable team might do that we've decided against.

HOW WE'LL KNOW IT'S WORKING

1-3 metrics, with current baseline and target.

The value of this format is entirely in its brevity — if it doesn't fit on one page, it's not a strategy yet, it's a wish list.


2. Playing to Win — Strategy Choice Cascade
Roger Martin's framework, best for: forcing genuinely hard choices rather than aspirational goals. Widely used at P&G and taught in most MBA strategy courses.

STRATEGY: [Product/Business Name]

1. WHAT IS OUR WINNING ASPIRATION?

Not "grow revenue" — the specific definition of what winning looks like and why it matters.

2. WHERE WILL WE PLAY?

- Which markets/segments/customers

- Which geographies

- Which channels

- Which stage of the customer journey

(Be as specific about where you WON'T play as where you will.)

3. HOW WILL WE WIN THERE?

The specific source of competitive advantage — cost, differentiation, network effects, distribution, speed. Not a list of good qualities — ONE clear theory of victory.

4. WHAT CAPABILITIES MUST BE IN PLACE?

The 3-5 things the org must be able to do exceptionally well to execute the "how will we win" — often reveals gaps.

5. WHAT MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS ARE REQUIRED?

What has to be true operationally — metrics tracked, processes, org structure, incentive alignment — to sustain this.

REVERSE STRESS-TEST

For each choice above, ask: "For this to be true, what would have to be true about the market/customer/competitor?" If those conditions are implausible, the choice is wrong.

This is deliberately the hardest template to fill out honestly — most teams write aspirations in box 1 and skip box 2 (where NOT to play), which defeats the purpose.


3. North Star Framework
Best for: aligning a team (especially growth/product-led orgs) around one metric that captures value delivered, not vanity growth.

PRODUCT: [Name]

NORTH STAR METRIC

The single metric that best captures the core value your product delivers to customers.

(e.g., not "signups" — "weekly active teams completing a core workflow")

WHY THIS METRIC

Why this one, not an adjacent metric — what makes it a true proxy for value delivered rather than just activity.

INPUT METRICS (the levers that move the North Star)

| Input Metric | Current | Target | Owning Team/Initiative |

|---|---|---|---|

HOW INPUTS LADDER UP

Brief causal logic — why moving each input actually moves the North Star (not just correlated).

GUARDRAIL METRICS

What must NOT regress while we chase the North Star (e.g., churn, support load, quality).

REVIEW CADENCE

How often this is revisited — weekly/monthly dashboard review, quarterly re-validation that it's still the right metric.


4. OKR-Based Strategy Doc
Best for: quarterly or annual planning with clear accountability, common in mid-to-large orgs (Google-popularized format).

STRATEGIC CONTEXT

2-3 sentences on the broader "why" this quarter/year's OKRs exist — link to company mission/strategy.

OBJECTIVE 1: [Qualitative, inspiring, time-bound]

- KR 1.1: [Measurable, specific number/threshold]

- KR 1.2: [Measurable]

- KR 1.3: [Measurable]

OBJECTIVE 2: [Qualitative, inspiring, time-bound]

- KR 2.1:

- KR 2.2:

(Repeat — most teams cap at 3-5 objectives to avoid diluted focus)

NON-OBJECTIVES

What we are explicitly deprioritizing this cycle, so a KR miss on something outside this list isn't treated as a failure.

DEPENDENCIES ACROSS TEAMS

Where this team's OKRs rely on another team's OKRs landing first.

CONFIDENCE LEVEL

Per objective — realistic self-assessed likelihood of hitting it (encourages ambitious KRs without punishing stretch goals as failures).


5. Vision & Strategy Narrative (Amazon-Style)
Best for: board decks, leadership buy-in, or any moment where you need people to feel the future state, not just read a plan. Narrative-first, in the spirit of Amazon's "no PowerPoint" 6-pager culture.

[Written in full prose, no bullets until the final section — the discipline of narrative writing forces clearer thinking than bullet points allow]

## The World As It Is

Honest description of the current market/customer/competitive reality, without spin.

## The World As It Should Be

The future state you're aiming to create — for the customer, specifically, not for the company.

## Why We Are Uniquely Positioned to Build This

What advantage, insight, asset, or timing gives you the right to win here — and why others haven't already done this.

## What We Will Do

The strategy itself, in narrative form — the sequence of moves, not just a list of features.

## What Could Go Wrong

Honest risk assessment — market risk, execution risk, competitive response.

## What Success Looks Like in [Timeframe]

Concrete, vivid description of the state you'll be in if this works.

---

[APPENDIX — the only place bullets are allowed]

- Key metrics & targets

- Resourcing requirements

- Timeline / major milestones


6. Go-to-Market Strategy Doc
Best for: launching a new product, feature, or entering a new market/segment.

PRODUCT/FEATURE: [Name]

LAUNCH TARGET: [Date]

MARKET OPPORTUNITY

Size, growth rate, why now — grounded in data where possible.

TARGET SEGMENT

Specific ICP (ideal customer profile) — not "everyone," the actual first-wave buyer.

POSITIONING

"[Product] is the [category] for [target segment] who need [need], unlike [alternative] which [limitation]."

PRICING & PACKAGING

How this is priced/packaged, and why — relative to alternatives and perceived value.

CHANNEL STRATEGY

How target customers will find/acquire this — direct sales, self-serve, partnerships, content, existing customer base.

LAUNCH SEQUENCE

- Pre-launch (beta, waitlist, early access)

- Launch moment (what happens day 1)

- Post-launch (expansion, iteration based on feedback)

SALES/CS ENABLEMENT

What internal teams need to sell/support this — training, collateral, FAQs.

SUCCESS METRICS

Adoption target, revenue target, and the timeframe each applies to.

COMPETITIVE RESPONSE

How competitors are likely to react, and how you'll respond.


7. Portfolio / Roadmap Strategy Doc
Best for: prioritizing across multiple product lines or a large backlog with genuinely limited resources — the document that explains why this and not that.

PORTFOLIO: [Product Line / Org Name]

PERIOD: [e.g., FY26 H1]

STRATEGIC PILLARS

The 3-5 themes everything in this portfolio should ladder up to. Anything that doesn't map to a pillar is a candidate for cutting.

INITIATIVE PRIORITIZATION

| Initiative | Pillar | Impact (H/M/L) | Effort (H/M/L) | Confidence | Decision |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

RESOURCE ALLOCATION

Rough % or headcount split across pillars/initiatives — makes tradeoffs visible rather than implicit.

WHAT WE'RE explicitly DEPRIORITIZING

Named initiatives that were considered and cut, with the reasoning — prevents the same idea resurfacing without new information.

SEQUENCING LOGIC

Why this order — dependencies, market timing, resource availability.

REVIEW TRIGGERS

Conditions that would cause this prioritization to be revisited before the next planning cycle (e.g., a competitor launch, a KR miss, new data).


8. Product Strategy Canvas (Single Product Area)
Best for: a PM setting strategy for one product/feature area within a larger company — narrower scope than company-wide strategy, more strategic than a single PRD.

PRODUCT AREA: [Name]

OWNER:

TIME HORIZON:

PROBLEM SPACE

The durable, underlying problem this area exists to solve — should outlive any single feature.

CUSTOMER INSIGHT

The non-obvious thing you know about the customer that shapes this strategy — from research, data, or support patterns.

STRATEGIC BETS

The 2-4 specific bets you're making about how to win in this space, each with:

- The bet

- Why you believe it

- What would prove it wrong

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Who else is solving this problem (directly or via workaround), and your point of differentiation.

ROADMAP THEMES (not feature list)

The 3-5 themes the next several sprints/quarters will ladder up to.

METRICS THAT MATTER

Leading indicators you'll watch to know if the bets are paying off, before lagging business metrics catch up.

KILL CRITERIA

What evidence would cause you to abandon a bet rather than double down on it.


Notes on Using These for The Sprint School
The interesting teaching contrast here is altitude and audience, not just format:

Lean One-Pager and North Star are built to be lived with — read weekly, drive daily decisions.
Playing to Win and Vision Narrative are built to be argued over once — a small number of intense working sessions to get buy-in, then referenced quarterly.
OKRs and Portfolio Strategy are built to be revisited on a cycle — quarterly rhythm baked into the format itself.

If you're teaching this alongside the PRD library, the natural progression for the cohort is: Strategy Canvas → OKRs → PRD — i.e., strategy sets the bet and the metric, OKRs turn it into accountable near-term targets, and the PRD is where a single initiative gets specified in buildable detail. Might be worth a diagram showing that funnel explicitly, since it's the piece most junior PMs get wrong — jumping straight to PRDs without ever articulating the bet they're actually testing.
