1. Opportunity Context

Target customer
Problem
Evidence

Opportunity size

Existing alternatives
Output: Opportunity brief.

2. Problem Reframing
Turn the opportunity into a clear design challenge.
Instead of:
"We need an inventory dashboard."
Reframe it as:
"How might we help multi-store retailers understand their inventory position without manually checking multiple systems?"
This keeps the team focused on the customer outcome, not a predetermined feature.
Output: How Might We statement.

3. Desired Outcomes
Define what success looks like for the customer.
Customer outcome
Know current stock position across all stores.
Business outcome
Increase adoption of multi-store inventory management.
Product outcome
Increase weekly usage of inventory visibility features.
Output: Outcome statements.

4. Solution Principles
Define the rules that should guide the solution.
For example:
The solution should:
✓ Reduce manual work
✓ Provide reliable information
✓ Work across multiple stores
✓ Be easy to understand
✓ Require minimal training
✓ Surface important exceptions
These become design constraints.

5. Solution Hypotheses
State what you believe the solution could accomplish.
We believe providing a unified real-time inventory view will reduce the time retailers spend reconciling stock across locations.
Then define:
Assumption → Solution → Expected behavior → Expected outcome

6. Solution Exploration
Explore different ways of solving the problem.
Don't immediately settle on the first idea.
Example:
Problem: Retailers don't know where stock is.
Possible approaches:
Central inventory dashboard
Automated stock alerts
Daily inventory summary
Cross-store search
Stock health score
Automated reconciliation
AI inventory assistant

17. Value Proposition
Define the value clearly.
A useful structure:
For [target customer] who [problem], our solution [solution] provides [benefit], unlike [alternative], because [differentiator].
Example:
For multi-store retailers who struggle to maintain accurate stock visibility, our inventory intelligence platform provides a unified view of stock across locations, unlike spreadsheets and disconnected POS systems.

Give users realistic tasks.
Example:

Misunderstandings

21. Solution Validation
Bring all evidence together.
Evaluate:
DESIRABILITY
     +
FEASIBILITY
     +
VIABILITY
     +
USABILITY
     =
VALIDATED SOLUTION
Importantly, validation doesn't mean everyone loved the prototype.
It means you have sufficient evidence to justify moving forward.

23. Trade-offs
Document what you're intentionally not optimizing for.
Example:
We will prioritize speed and simplicity over advanced customization in V1.
This becomes very important during execution.

27. Discovery Decision
At the end, make a deliberate decision:
🟢 Proceed
Evidence is strong enough to build.
🟡 Iterate
Solution shows promise but needs refinement.
🔵 Experiment Further
Important assumptions remain unvalidated.
🟠 Defer
Opportunity is valid but timing isn't right.
🔴 Stop
Evidence does not justify further investment.
