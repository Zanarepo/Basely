# Roadmap Template Library

A companion to the PRD and Strategy libraries — this is the layer between them. Strategy sets the bet, PRDs specify the build, and the roadmap is the communication artifact that shows sequencing over time to different audiences (exec, eng, sales, customers). The single biggest mistake in roadmapping is using one format for every audience — a roadmap for the board and a roadmap for engineering should rarely look identical.

---

## Decision Guide: Which Template Do I Use?

| If you're... | Use |
|---|---|
| Communicating to exec/board without overcommitting to dates | Now-Next-Later |
| Organizing work around customer/business themes rather than features | Theme-Based Roadmap |
| Committing to specific dates for sales, marketing, or ops coordination | Timeline / Release-Based Roadmap |
| Trying to keep the team focused on outcomes, not shipping features for their own sake | Outcome-Based Roadmap |
| Running a lean/continuous flow team without fixed sprint boundaries | Kanban-Style Roadmap |
| Prioritizing a large backlog with an explicit scoring method | GIST Roadmap |
| Directly tying roadmap items to OKRs for accountability | Goal-Linked Roadmap |
| Managing multiple products/product lines under one strategy | Portfolio Roadmap |
| Sharing roadmap externally with customers/prospects | Public-Facing Roadmap |

---

## 1. Now / Next / Later

The most widely used format for exec and customer-facing roadmaps because it communicates *intent and sequencing* without the false precision of committed dates. Popularized by ProdPad/Janna Bastow.

```
NOW (actively being built, this quarter)
- [Initiative] — [1-line outcome it delivers]
- [Initiative]

NEXT (defined, prioritized, starting soon — no fixed date)
- [Initiative]
- [Initiative]

LATER (directionally planned, not yet scoped in detail)
- [Initiative]
- [Initiative]

EXPLORING (validating whether this belongs on the roadmap at all)
- [Idea]
```

The discipline: nothing in "Later" should have a date attached — the moment you add a date, you've silently converted this into a commitment roadmap, which defeats its purpose.

---

## 2. Theme-Based Roadmap

Best for: organizing around customer/business problems rather than a feature list — keeps the roadmap legible even as individual features change.

```
THEME 1: [e.g., "Reduce time-to-first-value for new users"]
Why this theme: [1-2 sentences — the problem/opportunity]
- Initiative A (Now)
- Initiative B (Next)
- Initiative C (Later)

THEME 2: [e.g., "Enterprise readiness"]
Why this theme:
- Initiative A (Now)
- Initiative B (Next)

THEME 3: [...]
```

Themes should map back to your strategy doc's pillars (see the Portfolio Strategy template) — if a theme here doesn't trace to a strategic pillar, ask why it's on the roadmap.

---

## 3. Timeline / Release-Based Roadmap

Best for: contexts where specific dates genuinely matter — regulatory deadlines, contractual commitments, coordinated marketing launches, hardware/firmware cycles.

```
[Swimlane format — one row per team/product area, columns = time periods]

              Q1              Q2              Q3              Q4
Team A    [Feature] ────►  [Feature]       [Feature]
Team B                     [Feature] ───────────────►
Team C    [Feature]        [Feature]       [Feature]  [Feature]

RELEASE: v2.1 — [Target Date]
- Scope: [list]
- Dependencies: [list]
- Risk to date: [Low/Med/High + why]

RELEASE: v2.2 — [Target Date]
- Scope:
- Dependencies:
- Risk to date:
```

Use this format only when you're prepared to defend real dates — it invites the "why did this slip" conversation in a way Now/Next/Later doesn't. Good for internal engineering/ops use, riskier for external or exec-facing use unless dates are genuinely firm.

---

## 4. Outcome-Based Roadmap

Best for: teams trying to escape "feature factory" mode — every roadmap line is an outcome, with the feature(s) that deliver it listed underneath, not the other way around.

```
OUTCOME: [e.g., "New users complete onboarding without support contact"]
Current baseline: [metric]
Target: [metric]
Timeframe: [quarter]

Contributing initiatives:
- [Feature/change 1]
- [Feature/change 2]

---

OUTCOME: [next outcome]
Current baseline:
Target:
Timeframe:

Contributing initiatives:
- [Feature/change]
```

The test of whether this format is working: if you removed the "contributing initiatives" and just showed the outcomes to the team, would they still know roughly what to build? If not, the outcomes are too vague.

---

## 5. Kanban-Style Roadmap

Best for: lean/continuous-flow teams, especially small startups or platform teams without fixed sprint cadences.

```
BACKLOG          READY           IN PROGRESS       VALIDATING        DONE
[Idea]           [Scoped item]   [Item + owner]    [Item + metric    [Shipped item]
[Idea]           [Scoped item]                      being watched]
[Idea]
```

Unlike Now/Next/Later, this format emphasizes *flow and WIP limits* over time horizon — it's less about "when" and more about "what's actually moving." Works well pinned in a tool like Linear/Trello rather than a static doc, since it's meant to be live, not a point-in-time artifact.

---

## 6. GIST Roadmap (Goals, Ideas, Step-Projects, Tasks)

Itamar Gilad's framework — best for: teams with a large volume of ideas needing a structured, less-hierarchical prioritization method than a rigid OKR cascade.

```
GOALS (the few, durable, high-level objectives — reviewed quarterly/annually)
- Goal 1
- Goal 2

IDEAS (a living, continuously-groomed backlog — not all will be built)
| Idea | Linked Goal | Confidence | Impact Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|

STEP-PROJECTS (ideas promoted into small, time-boxed experiments — typically 1-6 weeks)
| Step-Project | Linked Idea | Success Criteria | Status |
|---|---|---|---|

TASKS (the day-to-day execution work within an active step-project)
[Standard sprint/task tracking — lives in your PM tool, not the roadmap doc itself]
```

The key discipline GIST enforces: nothing skips straight from "idea" to "large committed project" — everything must pass through a small validating step-project first, which naturally prevents big roadmap bets built on untested assumptions.

---

## 7. Goal-Linked Roadmap (OKR-Tied)

Best for: orgs that want the roadmap to be explicitly and visibly accountable to the quarter's OKRs, not just loosely inspired by them.

```
OBJECTIVE: [from your OKR doc]
  KR: [specific metric/target]

  Roadmap items supporting this KR:
  - [Initiative] — Owner: [name] — Status: [Now/Next/Later] — Confidence: [H/M/L]
  - [Initiative] — Owner: — Status: — Confidence:

OBJECTIVE: [next objective]
  KR:

  Roadmap items supporting this KR:
  - [Initiative]

UNMAPPED WORK
Anything on the roadmap that doesn't trace to a current OKR — flagged deliberately, so leadership can decide if it's justified (e.g., tech debt, compliance) or should be cut.
```

The "Unmapped Work" section is the most valuable part of this template — it's where roadmap bloat gets caught before it ships.

---

## 8. Portfolio Roadmap (Multi-Product)

Best for: managing roadmaps across multiple products or product lines under a shared strategy — relevant if Sellytics and Miniventory ever need to be shown on one board.

```
                    Q1              Q2              Q3              Q4
PRODUCT A       [Theme/Initiative] [Theme]         [Theme]
PRODUCT B       [Theme]            [Theme]         [Theme]         [Theme]
SHARED/         [Platform work benefiting both — e.g., shared auth, billing]
PLATFORM

CROSS-PRODUCT DEPENDENCIES
| Dependency | From → To | Risk if delayed |
|---|---|---|

RESOURCE CONTENTION
Where the same team/skillset is needed across products in the same period — flagged explicitly so it's a leadership decision, not a surprise.
```

---

## 9. Public-Facing Roadmap

Best for: sharing externally with customers or prospects — deliberately vaguer than internal roadmaps, designed to build trust and gather feedback without becoming a contractual promise.

```
SHIPPED RECENTLY
- [Feature] — [1-line customer benefit, not technical description]

IN PROGRESS
- [Feature/theme] — [1-line benefit]

PLANNED
- [Theme, not specific feature] — [why customers should care]

UNDER CONSIDERATION
- [Idea] — [Vote/feedback mechanism, e.g., "upvote this"]

Note: dates are intentionally omitted. If your company culture requires quarter-level indicators, use "Later this year" rather than a specific quarter — protects against the reputational cost of public slippage.
```

## Notes on Using These for The Sprint School

The teaching point that ties this library to the Strategy and PRD libraries is **traceability across altitude**: a well-run product org can point to any single roadmap line and answer three questions instantly —

1. Which strategic pillar or OKR does this serve? *(Strategy doc / Goal-Linked Roadmap)*
2. What outcome is it actually trying to produce? *(Outcome-Based Roadmap)*
3. What's the buildable spec once it's prioritized? *(PRD)*

Most roadmap dysfunction in real teams isn't a formatting problem — it's a **broken link** somewhere in that chain: a roadmap item nobody can trace back to a goal, or a PRD getting written for something that was never actually prioritized. Worth building one worked example (e.g., a Sellytics theme) that walks Strategy Canvas → Goal-Linked Roadmap → PRD end-to-end for the cohort, since that's the connective tissue most junior PMs never see modeled explicitly.
