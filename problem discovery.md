Industry / market context
Business environment
Current workflows

User segments

User journey

Manual work
Delays

Confusion

Lack of information

Question: What is making the current experience difficult?

6. Jobs-to-be-Done

Understand what the customer is ultimately trying to accomplish.

Format:

When [situation], I want to [motivation/action], so I can [desired outcome].

Example:

When I am managing multiple stores, I want to see inventory across all locations, so I can make accurate purchasing decisions.

Question: What is the user actually trying to accomplish?

7. Root Cause Analysis

Go beyond the obvious symptom.

Useful techniques:

5 Whys
Fishbone / Ishikawa
Cause-and-effect analysis
Process analysis
Data analysis

Example:

Problem: Stockouts

↓ Why?

Inventory isn't updated consistently.

↓ Why?

Store employees record sales manually.

↓ Why?

The current system isn't available offline.

Now you've moved from the symptom to a potential root cause.

8. Problem Evidence

Separate assumptions from evidence.

Capture:

Interview evidence
Quantitative data
Analytics
Support tickets
Sales data
Customer complaints
Survey results
Observational evidence
Market evidence

A useful classification is:

Fact → Evidence → Assumption → Hypothesis

9. Problem Frequency & Severity

Determine how significant the problem is.

Evaluate:

Frequency

How often does it happen?

Severity

How painful is it?

Reach

How many users experience it?

Impact

What happens because of it?

For example:

Problem	Frequency	Severity	Reach
Inventory mismatch	Daily	High	70%
Slow reporting	Weekly	Medium	40%
Poor customization	Monthly	Low	15%
10. Existing Solutions / Workarounds

Understand what users currently do.

They may use:

Excel
WhatsApp
Paper
Competitor products
Spreadsheets
Manual processes
Multiple systems
Personal workarounds

This is extremely important.

A workaround tells you:

"The user already cares enough about this problem to spend effort solving it."

11. Competitive / Alternative Analysis

Look at how the problem is currently addressed.

Analyze:

Direct competitors
Indirect competitors
Alternative solutions
Substitute products
Manual processes
"Do nothing"

The last one is important.

Your real competitor isn't always another SaaS product.

Sometimes it's:

Excel + WhatsApp + human effort.

12. Problem Statement

Now synthesize everything into a concise problem statement.

A good structure:

[User] needs a way to [job/task] because [problem/root cause], resulting in [negative outcome].

Example:

Multi-store retail owners need a reliable way to maintain real-time inventory visibility because stock movements are tracked inconsistently across locations, resulting in stock discrepancies, stockouts and poor purchasing decisions.

13. Opportunity / Problem Hypotheses

Convert your findings into hypotheses.

Example:

We believe multi-store retailers struggle with inventory visibility.

Because inventory is maintained separately across locations.

This causes purchasing errors and stockouts.

We will know this is true if interviews and operational data show that inventory reconciliation is a recurring high-impact problem.

14. Problem Validation

Finally, validate whether the problem is real, important and worth solving.

Ask:

Is this a real problem?
Who experiences it?
How frequently?
How severe is it?
What does it cost?
What are users doing today?
Are they actively looking for solutions?
Are they willing to change?
Is the problem strategically relevant?
Is there sufficient evidence?
